94% of popular websites split a button across a foldable's hinge

We opened 79 of the most visited websites on a simulated Samsung Galaxy Z Fold and checked what lands under the hinge. Even Google's search box is cut in half. Here is what we found, and how to fix it.

94%split a button, link or form field with the hinge
70%are not foldable-ready (score below 90/100)
72average score out of 100 in book posture
65%showed a cookie banner sitting on the hinge

Scores are mediocre across the board

No site reached 100. Book posture (screen unfolded like a book, vertical hinge) is where most sites break: the hinge runs down the middle of centered layouts. Tabletop posture (half-folded like a laptop) does better, at 93 on average.

Methodology

  • Tool: npx foldjs audit (fold.js 0.2), headless Chrome with an Android user agent, on October 28, 2026, from France.
  • Device: Samsung Galaxy Z Fold inner screen, 690×829 CSS pixels in book posture and 829×690 in tabletop posture, with a zero-width fold line as on real devices.
  • What is checked: the first screen of each home page. An issue is an element whose visible part crosses the fold line. Split buttons, links and form fields are critical; text, modals, floating elements, videos and carousels are warnings; images are minor.
  • Score: 100 minus 6 per critical issue, 3 per warning and 1 per minor issue, per posture; the site score is the average of both postures. 90 and above counts as foldable-ready.
  • Cookie banners: a first pass was run as a normal visitor (that is where the 65% figure comes from). The results above come from a second pass where the audit closed consent banners automatically, rejecting when possible. About a dozen banners could not be closed and are part of the result.
  • Sites list: 100 of the most visited global and French websites. 21 sites showed a bot check or a country selection page instead of the real site and are left out.
  • Limits: scores reflect one moment, one location and one device size. Sites change often; run the audit yourself to check today's result.
